How this calculator works

The calculator uses the same arithmetic that goes on your written ClearKarat slip. It takes your gross weight, removes the weight of stones or dirt, applies the purity of your gold, and multiplies by the 24K gold rate per gram that you enter. If your gold is pledged, it also subtracts your loan payoff so you can see the money that would come to you.

Why the number is an estimate

Your real figure depends on the weighed net weight, the tested purity and the rate fixed in front of you when your gold is weighed. Gold rates change through the day. Use the estimate to prepare, then confirm with a written slip. For a deeper explanation, read how old gold is valued.

Questions

Straight answers

How does ClearKarat value my gold?

We weigh your gold on a scale whose display faces you, take out the weight of any stones, lac or dirt, test the purity while you watch, and multiply the net weight by the purity and the day's gold rate. Each of those numbers goes on its own line of a written slip before you decide anything.

Why are stones and dirt weighed out?

Stones, lac, enamel and dirt have weight but are not gold, so they cannot be paid for as gold. Removing them gives the net weight, which is the weight your price is based on. The deduction appears as its own line on the slip so you can see exactly what was taken out.

Does the gold rate change during the day?

Yes, gold rates move through the day. The rate that counts is the one on your written slip, which is the rate used when your gold is weighed and tested in front of you. Ask us which rate is being used and where it comes from.
