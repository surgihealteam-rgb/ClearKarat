How old gold is valued: the formula explained

Learn how old gold is priced in India: net weight, purity and the day's rate, with a worked example you can check yourself.

Updated 3 Nov 2026 · ClearKarat

Key takeaways
  • Value = net weight x purity x the day's 24K rate per gram.
  • Net weight is what remains after stones, lac and dirt are removed.
  • Making charges you paid when buying jewellery are generally not paid back when you sell.
  • Ask which rate is used, when it was fixed, and for every deduction by name.

The basic formula

Almost every fair gold price comes from one line of arithmetic:

Value = net weight × purity × the day's 24K gold rate per gram

Purity is the karat divided by 24. So 22K gold is 22/24, or about 91.6%. 18K is 75%. 24K is about 99.9%.

A worked example

StepExample
Gross weight on the scale12.40 g
Less: stones, lac, dirt0.40 g
Net weight12.00 g
Purity22K = 22/24 = 0.9167
Fine gold12.00 × 0.9167 = 11.00 g
24K rate per gram (illustrative)₹ 10,000
Value11.00 × 10,000 = ₹ 1,10,000

The rate in this example is a round number for illustration. It is not today's rate. Enter today's rate in our gold value calculator to try your own numbers.

Why net weight, not gross weight

Stones, lac, enamel, thread and dirt weigh something but are not gold. A fair buyer removes that weight first and shows it to you as its own line. If a buyer weighs everything together and never mentions stones, ask for the deduction in writing.

Why you do not get making charges back

When you bought the jewellery, the price included making charges and taxes. When you sell, buyers generally pay for the gold content. This is why old jewellery sells for less than you paid even when the gold rate has risen.

Which rate is used

Gold rates move through the day. Ask which rate is being used, where it comes from and when it was fixed. The rate on your written slip should be the one used when your gold was weighed, not a rate quoted days ago.

Check before you agree

  1. See the weight on a display that faces you.
  2. See the purity test and ask what it showed.
  3. Ask for every deduction, by name, on paper.
  4. Redo the arithmetic with the calculator.
  5. Say no if any line does not make sense.
